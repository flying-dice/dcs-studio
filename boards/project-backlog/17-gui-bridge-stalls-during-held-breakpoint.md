# A held mission breakpoint stalls the GUI bridge entirely

Found by card 03's live session (journalled there, and on
[#65](https://github.com/flying-dice/dcs-studio/issues/65)): while a mission
breakpoint is held, the mission bridge stays responsive (`debug_state` in
24–108 ms) but **every call to the GUI bridge on `:25569` times out at the
30 s server deadline for the entire duration of the pause**, spamming
`deadline has elapsed` into the log. Pausing at a breakpoint makes the status
bar and every GUI-side feature look broken for as long as the user inspects
state — which is the whole point of a breakpoint.

Likely seam: the GUI bridge drains its queue per `onSimulationFrame`, and a
held mission breakpoint (or a paused sim generally — card 04's session showed
the same symptom from the briefing screen for the *mission* pump) stops the
frame callbacks the pump depends on. Confirm the mechanism before fixing.

Related: card 04 / #32 — same family of "socket state and pump liveness
disagree" problems; a fix here should be designed with that card's
`dcsTime`-freshness idea in view rather than independently.

## Checklist

- [x] Confirm the mechanism: does `onSimulationFrame` stop firing while a mission breakpoint is held / the sim is paused? — **yes, it stops; the transport stays fine**
- [x] Decide the fix (pump GUI queue from a callback that survives a pause, or fail fast with a "sim paused" answer instead of a 30 s timeout) — **fail fast: every drain stamps a pump clock, and a request arriving against a stale stamp is answered `-32002 sim not pumping` immediately**
- [x] Live-verify: hold a breakpoint, confirm GUI-side calls either work or fail fast with a truthful message — **verified live 2026-07-30 (session 4): -32002 in 1-23 ms during the hold, mission bridge serving debug_state in 13 ms meanwhile, full recovery within 0.5 s of debug_continue**

- **opus-fastfail** (2026-07-30T10:40:00.000Z): **Took checklist item 2 and built the fix the previous session's evidence dictates: report, do not repair.** The measurement was unambiguous — during a hold the GUI transport is perfectly healthy (`/health` 1-2 ms) and only the Lua-side drain stops, and the stall lifts 3 ms after resume — so there is nothing wrong to fix and the entire user-visible defect is the 30 s wait. The mechanism: every pump now stamps a "last drained" instant on the server's per-server `AppData` (`bridge/crates/bridge-core/src/jsonrpc/server.rs:148-165`), stamped inside `drain_queue` (`bridge/crates/bridge-core/src/jsonrpc/server.rs:684-711`), and the HTTP/WS layer checks that stamp *before* parking a request in the queue (`accept_request`, `bridge/crates/bridge-core/src/jsonrpc/server.rs:755-795`). A request arriving against a stale stamp is answered immediately with a new JSON-RPC error, `-32002 "sim not pumping"` (`bridge/crates/bridge-core/src/jsonrpc/mod.rs:26-38`), whose `data` names the observable fact and the idle time. Both transports refuse identically: `POST /rpc` returns 200 with the envelope (`bridge/crates/bridge-core/src/jsonrpc/server.rs:830-848`) and the WebSocket writes the refusal from inside the read loop so it cannot be reordered behind a later frame's reply (`bridge/crates/bridge-core/src/jsonrpc/server.rs:882-910`).
- **opus-fastfail** (2026-07-30T10:42:00.000Z): **The design question that actually needed thinking through was the debugger, and the wiring turned out to make an exemption unnecessary — every pump already funnels through one place.** `drain_queue` is reached by `process_rpc` (the GUI hook's `onSimulationFrame`, `bridge/hook/DcsStudio.lua:101-105`, and the mission state's model-time timer, `bridge/crates/bridge-mission/lua/mission_init.lua:181-196`) *and* by `process_global_queue`, which is what `jsonrpc.process_queue` exposes and what both `DBG.pump` wirings call (`bridge/hook/DcsStudio.lua:72-76`, `bridge/crates/bridge-mission/lua/mission_init.lua:171-178`). The engine's pause loop calls `D.pump()` every `DRAIN_INTERVAL_SECONDS` = 50 ms while it holds the sim thread (`bridge/crates/bridge-core/lua/debug_engine.lua:567-591`). So stamping inside `drain_queue` means **the bridge the debug engine is serving keeps stamping itself throughout a held breakpoint** and never fast-fails its own `debug_state`/`debug_continue`, while the bridge nobody is pumping goes stale — precisely the asymmetry the live session measured (mission answering in 6-40 ms, GUI unable to dispatch at all). No per-bridge exemption, no special-casing the mission env, and the property is pinned by a test rather than left as a comment.
- **opus-fastfail** (2026-07-30T10:44:00.000Z): **Threshold: 2 s, with the reasoning kept in the constant's docs (`bridge/crates/bridge-core/src/jsonrpc/server.rs:66-87`).** The floor is the slowest *healthy* drain interval: the GUI hook drains per frame (16 ms at 60 fps, 100 ms at a miserable 10) and the mission state per 0.1 s of model time, so 2 s is ~20x the worst legitimate gap — a heavy frame cannot reach it. The ceiling is the 30 s deadline it exists to avoid: 2 s is 15x faster feedback and sits under the extension's own 2 s status poll, so a paused sim is reported on the first or second poll instead of never. It is overridable per server (`pump_stale_ms` in the config table) and `0` disables the refusal — that knob is what let the existing integration harness keep testing the genuine timeout paths unchanged, and it is a real escape hatch. **The honest cost, journalled deliberately:** a multi-second gap that is *not* a pause — a mission load, or a `repl_export`/`db_export` serialising a huge table on the sim thread — also reads as stalled. That is why the message says the queue has not been drained and that the bridge will serve again when pumped, rather than asserting "the sim is paused": the handler cannot distinguish those causes, and claiming one it has not observed would be the same class of mistake as the misleading timeout. A truthful "not being served right now" in 2 s still beats an indistinguishable silence for 30. Notifications are deliberately **not** refused — no caller waits and no deadline burns, so they stay queued and their side effects run on the frame the sim resumes.
